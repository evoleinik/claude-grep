## Criteria — FIXED BEFORE OPTIONS (do not revise once a preference is on the table)

1. **Measured hit@1 on the 17-query corpus.** Primary. A change must move it by
   more than the run-to-run noise, which measured +/-1 query between identical
   configs on different distractor samples.
2. **Query latency.** `-s` is 1.4s today. Anything that pushes an interactive
   search past ~3s is a real cost to daily use.
3. **Entities added.** New files, lanes, models and config knobs all have to be
   maintained. Fewer is better at equal gain.
4. **Mechanically verifiable.** The change must be checkable by the existing
   bench, not by eyeballing results.

## Fact gathered before ranking the options (Claude)

Raising the candidate pool to 500 chunks finds two of the three "missing"
targets at session-rank 36 and 82. Only `youthful-fermi` is genuinely absent.

This changes the ranking. Six of the seven failures are **already retrieved and
badly ordered**, not missing. Anything that only widens the pool raises hit@10
and cannot move hit@1. The lever is discrimination at the top.

Supporting evidence: the same configuration scores hit@1 13/17 on the 167-session
subset and 10/17 on the 2164-project index. The signal is adequate in a small
pool and degrades with the number of competitors.

## Options

### A. Cross-encoder or LLM rerank of the top ~20 sessions
Score each shortlisted session against the query with a model that sees both.
- **Strongest counterargument.** It is the standard answer for "right document
  is in the pool, wrong order", and it would very likely work. But 20 scored
  passages on box is seconds, against a 1.4s budget, and criterion 2 is not a
  detail — this tool is used interactively, dozens of times a day.
- **Failure mode.** A second model to calibrate and watch. We already have the
  answer-judge lesson: a judge installed once drifts, and nobody notices until a
  scheduled check catches it. This adds that liability to the search path.

### B. Query-term coverage reranking
Embed each content term of the query separately. Score a session by how many
distinct query terms it matches strongly, not just by summed similarity.
- **Strongest counterargument.** It is not a standard technique, it is my
  invention, and short-string embeddings are noisy. It may measure nothing.
- **Failure mode.** Silently hurts the queries that already work, by rewarding
  sessions that mention every term shallowly over the one that discusses the
  actual subject. `admin merge` is the case it should fix and also the case
  where breadth-over-depth is most likely to backfire.

### C. Query expansion into corpus vocabulary
Rewrite the paraphrase into likely index terms before embedding.
- **Strongest counterargument.** It directly targets the one true miss, where
  the user's words share no vocabulary with the corpus. But it needs an LLM per
  query (latency, criterion 2) or a hand-kept synonym map (brittle, criterion 3).
- **Failure mode.** A wrong expansion poisons a query that would otherwise have
  worked. The damage is invisible: you get confident wrong results, not an error.

## Facts that would change this ranking

- If B measures flat or negative on the bench, A becomes the recommendation and
  the latency cost has to be accepted or paid on the GPU.
- If a rerank can be batched into ONE model call under ~1s, A's main objection
  disappears and it should win on strength of evidence.
- If the three rank-0 cases turn out to share a cause (all tool-output heavy),
  a targeted fix may beat all three general ones.

## B measured and rejected — 2026-09-10 (Claude)

    baseline   hit@1 10  hit@3 11  hit@10 14  MRR 0.65   1.4s
    coverage   hit@1  6  hit@3 10  hit@10 11  MRR 0.48   2.9s

Worse on every criterion. It failed in precisely the way its stated failure
mode predicted: rewarding sessions that mention every query term shallowly over
the one that discusses the subject. Writing the failure mode down before
measuring is what made this a five-minute read rather than an argument.

Reverted. Per the criteria, A is now the recommendation, and its latency
objection has to be answered rather than waved away.

## Length bias ruled out — 2026-09-10 (Claude)

Hypothesis: sum-of-top-3 gives longer sessions more chances at a lucky chunk.
For the `lucid-shaw` failure, of the 59 sessions ranked above gold, only 3 have
more chunks and the median is identical to gold's. Not the cause.

So those sessions genuinely embed closer to the query than the right one does.
That is a discrimination limit of the embedding model, not an artifact of how
scores are combined. No amount of re-weighting existing vectors fixes it.

## Decision — 2026-09-10

Stop here at hit@1 10/17, MRR 0.65, 1.4s.

The two cheap levers are spent: coverage reranking measured worse, and length
normalisation has nothing to correct. What remains is A, a model that reads
query and passage together. That is likely to work and cannot be free: on box
CPU it is seconds per query against a 1.4s budget, and it adds a second model to
calibrate and watch.

Recommended next step IF this is picked up again, in order:
1. Measure A offline on the GPU before building it. 17 queries x 20 candidates
   through gemma4 on an L4, scoring hit@1. Cheap, and it settles whether the
   gain justifies the cost. Do not implement first.
2. Only if it wins clearly, ship it as opt-in (`--rerank`), so the default path
   stays at 1.4s and the slow path is a deliberate choice.

What this session did NOT establish: whether hit@1 above ~60% is reachable at
all on a 2164-project corpus with a 300M embedding model. The subset scores
13/17 and the full index 10/17 on identical code, so some of the gap is corpus
size rather than anything fixable in ranking.

## A measured and rejected — 2026-09-10 (Claude)

Ceiling first: gold is in the top-20 pool for 14/17, so a perfect reranker
scores hit@1 14. Nothing came close.

    baseline (no rerank)          hit@1 10  hit@3 11  hit@10 14  MRR 0.650
    gemma4:e4b   [batched]        hit@1 11  hit@3 12  hit@10 14  MRR 0.693
    gemma3:27b   [batched]        hit@1  5  hit@3 11  hit@10 14  MRR 0.510
    gemma3:27b   [per-candidate]  hit@1  5  hit@3  9  hit@10 13  MRR 0.438

The best variant gains ONE query. That is inside the run-to-run noise fixed in
the criteria before any option was written, and it costs a model call per
search. It does not clear the bar.

Two things worth keeping:
- The 27B model scored WORSE than the 4B, in both modes. Bigger did not mean
  better at judging relevance here, and the per-candidate mode — the one meant
  to be most accurate — was the worst of all. Do not assume a larger judge.
- Criterion 1 was written as "must move hit@1 by more than the noise". Without
  that written down first, +1 with a bigger model would have read as a win.

## Decision — 2026-09-10 (revised)

Ship nothing further. hit@1 stays 10/17, MRR 0.65, 1.4s.

Both candidate levers are now measured and rejected: coverage reranking (mine)
made it worse, LLM reranking (the standard answer) gained one query inside the
noise. The ceiling analysis says even a perfect reranker over this candidate
pool tops out at 14/17, so the remaining headroom is small and expensive.

What would change this: a better embedding model, since that is what decides
which sessions reach the pool at all. Not a reranker over the pool we have.
